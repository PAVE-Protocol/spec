## Rights in human judgment

PAVE's technology and the human judgment represented through it are distinct.

FRUIT's ownership of the PAVE protocol does not, by itself, establish ownership of a contributor's source material, knowledge records, identity, or represented judgment.

Likewise, permission to access PAVE technology does not automatically grant permission to use a particular person's judgment.

Applicable contributor and implementation agreements must address:

- **Source material:** What human knowledge, records, or other materials may be used and what judgment they represent.
- **Authorization:** Who may consult the resulting Lens, for which purposes, and under what conditions.
- **Attribution:** How the human source of a judgment contribution is identified.
- **Usage records:** What information about authorized consultations may be recorded or made available.
- **Compensation:** Any arrangements through which contributors receive value from the use of their represented judgment.

Permission to consult a Lens remains separate from authority to act on its contribution.

Attribution alone does not establish consent, endorsement, compensation, or responsibility for an AI system's actions.

## Economic participation

PAVE is designed to support a model in which people can participate economically when their represented judgment is used by AI systems.

Rather than requiring human expertise to be treated solely as material for training AI, PAVE provides an architecture through which represented judgment can remain identifiable and available for authorized consultation during an AI system's operation.

This creates a foundation for arrangements through which contributors may receive compensation or other value from authorized uses of their judgment.

Specific compensation structures, licensing terms, and usage-record requirements depend on the applicable agreements.

Attribution does not automatically create a payment obligation or guarantee earnings.
